# Research brief

**Question:** Is practice testing better than restudying for students' delayed exam performance?

## Answer

The evidence is mixed: in an undergraduate cognitive psychology course, brief daily review questions improved end-of-semester final exam performance only for content also covered on a unit exam, not for content covered only on daily review [E1]. In contrast, across four experiments using worked examples, testing did not improve delayed test performance relative to restudying, and the authors concluded the testing advantage over rereading may not generalize to problem-solving skill acquisition [E2][E4]. Thus practice testing appears better than restudying for some course-exam content but not for delayed problem-solving performance in these studies [E1][E2][E4].

## Key findings

- Retrieval practice via brief daily review questions improved final exam performance only for content covered on both daily review and unit exam [E1].
- Items covered only on daily review did not differ from items covered on neither [E1].
- Across four experiments with worked examples, testing did not improve delayed test performance relative to restudy [E2][E4].
- The testing advantage over rereading may not generalize to problem-solving skill acquisition [E2].

## Limits and conflicting evidence

- The sources disagree in direction: E1 found partial benefits of retrieval practice for course exam content, whereas E2/E4 found no testing effect for delayed problem-solving performance [E1][E2][E4].
- The difference may reflect task and setting: E1 involved an undergraduate cognitive psychology course with daily review questions and a final exam, while E2/E4 involved worked examples and problem-solving domains and populations [E1][E2][E4].
- E1's benefit was partial and limited to content also covered on a unit exam [E1].

## Open questions

- The evidence does not settle whether practice testing beats restudying for delayed exam performance across other course content, student populations, or problem-solving tasks beyond those studied [E1][E2][E4].
- The evidence does not establish the mechanism or optimal timing and format of testing needed to produce delayed exam benefits [E1][E2][E4].
